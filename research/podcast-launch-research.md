# Podcast Launch Research: Two Shows, Two Audiences

Research date: November 1, 2026
Purpose: Findings and a launch playbook for two separate podcasts, one on AI and one on crypto. Each section stands on its own. Method for both: adoption and sentiment surveys, Apple Podcasts charts (US, Nov 1, 2026), YouTube view comparisons by lane (scraped from YouTube search on Nov 1, 2026), search trends, and the people already succeeding in each space.

# Part 1: The AI Podcast

## 1.1 Lane recommendation

**Lane: AI for the curious, anxious, and busy.** You are the translator between the AI labs and normal life. Not an advocate, not an engineer, not a doomer. Honest about risks, relentlessly practical about what to do next.

Why this lane:
- It is the largest pool. Most adults use AI a little, a third have not touched it, and only one in ten are heavy users. The mass audience is light users plus worried non-users.
- It is under-served. Most existing AI shows are built for insiders (engineers, founders, investors) or are pure daily news.
- It is what the market is rewarding right now. The #1 US technology podcast on Apple today is Machine Gods, a three-week-old, video-first show built for a general audience.

Why not "advocacy": 52% of Americans are more concerned than excited about AI and only 9% are more excited. On the same channels in the same month, optimism-framed videos get thousands of views where warning-framed videos get millions. A cheerleader fights the room. A guide who takes the risks seriously and still shows people how to benefit gets the reach.

Why not "AI for fluent users": the insider shows (Latent Space, No Priors, Dwarkesh, How I AI) are real businesses but reach 5K to 150K per episode, versus 1M to 20M for general-audience AI content. Their hosts also have insider access a new host cannot match.

## 1.6 Findings: competition and monetization

- 4.7M+ podcasts exist; 30% to 45% of new feeds each day are likely AI-generated slop. Generic daily AI news with no point of view is the most saturated format.
- 62% of weekly podcast listeners see AI as a threat to podcast credibility; 85% (UK) disapprove of AI hosts. A real human with a clear point of view is a differentiator.
- YouTube CPM estimates for AI content (2026): AI tool tutorials and software reviews $10 to $28; B2B and SaaS automation $15 to $28; AI in finance $15 to $25; AI news $8 to $20; faceless AI automation $4 to $12 (saturated). Practical "how to" segments monetize best; sense-making segments bring the reach.
- Fast-growth precedent: Nate Herk went from zero to several hundred thousand subscribers in under a year on practical, no-code AI tutorials, and his YouTube audience fed a paid community that reached 300K+ members in 17 months.

## 1.7 Launch playbook

**Positioning statement.** The show for people who use AI a little, worry about it a lot, and want one trusted voice to tell them what matters this week, what it means for their job and money, and what to actually do.

**Three content pillars, ranked by audience size**

1. **Jobs, money, and "am I behind?"** The single biggest driver in every survey and every view count. Episode shapes: "What AI means for [job] in 2027," "I asked 10 people who use AI at work what actually changed," "The honest guide to AI side income," "You're not behind yet: the 20-minute catch-up."
2. **Sense-making on the week's big story, with a point of view.** Bubble or no bubble, whistleblowers, model launches, lawsuits, policy. Not a headline roundup; a take on why it matters to a normal person. This is the Whittemore and Machine Gods format, and it is what keeps people subscribed between big episodes.
3. **"How I actually use it."** Short, concrete, screen-recorded if video. One tool, one job, one outcome per segment. Beginner tutorials pull millions and carry the highest CPM.

**Format and cadence**

- Video-first on YouTube, audio distributed everywhere. Clips are the discovery engine (Diary of a CEO clips pull 3M to 8M each).
- Twice weekly if possible: one interview or deep dive, one news-with-a-take. Weekly minimum. Daily holds chart position but is the most crowded format for a newcomer.
- Episode length: 45 to 75 minutes for interviews; 15 to 25 minutes for the news-and-take episode (the AI Daily Brief runs a 5-minute headlines block plus a 10-minute analysis block).
- Two hosts or a host plus a rotating "normal person" co-host tests well: Hard Fork, Machine Gods, and AI For Humans all run as conversations between two people.

**Titles that work in this lane** (patterns from the top performers): stakes plus clarity. "[Thing], clearly explained." "What nobody is telling you about [event]." "You're not behind (yet)." "What [event] means for your job." "I tried [tool] for a week so you don't have to." "The honest case for and against [thing]."

**Guest strategy**

- You cannot book Hinton or Amodei in year one. Borrow credibility sideways: practitioners in ordinary jobs who have changed how they work (teachers, nurses, realtors, lawyers, small-business owners, recruiters). This gives you a repeatable "AI for [profession]" series where demand is proven and supply is thin.
- Mid-tier AI creators cross-promote readily (Matt Wolfe and Nate Herk appear on each other's channels).
- Skeptics and critics (the Ed Zitron, Karen Hao, Gary Marcus type) draw as many views as boosters and signal honesty.

**Distribution**

- Post the full episode on YouTube, then 3 to 6 vertical clips per episode on Shorts, TikTok, and Reels within 48 hours.
- Title and thumbnail for the curious viewer, not the insider: no model version numbers, no jargon in the first five words.
- Build an email list from day one; every top AI creator monetizes a newsletter or community alongside the show.

**What to avoid**

- Pure advocacy or optimism as the brand. The data says it caps reach.
- Copying Latent Space, Dwarkesh, or No Priors. You cannot win on insider access.
- Generic daily news without a point of view. Highest slop competition.
- Narrow demographic niches with no proven demand yet ("AI for seniors").
- Jargon-first titles. The curious viewer scrolls past "GPT-6.1 Sol benchmark deep dive."

**First ten episode ideas**

1. You're not behind (yet): the 30-minute AI catch-up for normal people
2. What the $6B AI bubble debate actually means for your retirement account
3. I asked a teacher, a nurse, a realtor, and a lawyer how AI changed their week
4. The honest guide to making money with AI (what works, what's a scam)
5. "AI will take your job": a calm look at the real numbers
6. How I use AI to run my life: email, money, health, kids
7. The whistleblowers: what AI insiders are warning about, in plain English
8. AI agents, clearly explained (and the one thing you should try this week)
9. Is AI making us dumber? What the research says
10. The week in AI, translated: what mattered and what was noise

# Part 2: The Crypto Podcast

## 2.1 Lane recommendation: two framings

The research supports two viable framings. Both are written up in full below so you can choose.

**Framing A: "Money and the system."** A show about what is happening to your money, where Bitcoin and crypto are one of the answers rather than the premise. Inflation, debt, the dollar, gold, stablecoins, prediction markets, scams, and the politics of money. This is the framing with the widest reach and the least dependence on the price cycle. It is the model of the top-charting crypto-native show today (Coin Stories, #13 on Apple's US Business News chart), and it is the angle that general shows use when they book crypto guests for millions of views.

**Framing B: "Crypto-first, macro and sound money."** A straight crypto show, built on the strongest crypto-native lane: Bitcoin as a response to the broken financial system, with market context, big-name interviews, and practical protection. This is the Coin Stories, What Bitcoin Did, and Pomp model. It reaches a smaller but more loyal and more sponsor-friendly audience, and its growth is tied to the price cycle.

In both framings, the host is an honest guide, not a promoter. The audience's defining trait is distrust, and the content that reaches furthest is content that takes that distrust seriously.

## 2.6 Findings: monetization and risk

- Crypto YouTube CPM estimates: $2 to $8 in bear phases, $10 to $30 in bull phases. Sponsorship CPMs are quoted at $40 to $300 and swing 50% to 100% with the market.
- Token projects and DeFi protocols often want to pay creators in their own tokens. Promoting a token that later collapses is a reputational and legal exposure.
- Crypto is a YMYL ("Your Money or Your Life") category on YouTube. Finance videos need disclaimers and face demonetization risk; sponsor disclosures are mandatory.
- Safe sponsor categories: hardware wallets, tax software, exchanges with real compliance, financial education. The practical-protection pillar below is the best fit for these.
- The Delphi Digital finding is the key planning fact: crypto creator audiences spike at price tops. Revenue tied to viewership will follow the cycle. Build a newsletter, community, or product that does not.

## 2.7 Launch playbook, Framing A: "Money and the system"

**Positioning statement.** The show for people who feel the financial system is not working for them, distrust banks and crypto exchanges alike, and want one honest voice to explain what is happening to their money and where Bitcoin and the new rails fit.

**Content pillars, ranked by reach**

1. **Scandals, hacks, and "how it actually worked."** The largest crypto-adjacent audience wants to understand the heist, not buy the coin. Cover every major event from the outside, plainly, within 48 hours. The Binance hack drew 1M+ per video in a day for channels that did this.
2. **Inflation, debt, the dollar, gold, and where Bitcoin fits.** The angle big general shows use when they book Raoul Pal, Saylor, and Armstrong for 2M to 6M views. Brunell's "why everything costs more" episodes outperform her crypto interviews.
3. **The new rails: stablecoins and prediction markets.** Stablecoins are now law and above $300B. Prediction markets doubled users in a year and are pulling in sports and politics audiences, including women, who never cared about crypto. Midterm-week content is doing 400K to 680K.
4. **Practical protection.** Self-custody, exchange risk, taxes, avoiding scams, what to do when a wallet or exchange fails. Smaller reach, highest trust, best sponsor fit.

**Format and cadence**

- Video-first on YouTube, audio everywhere. Weekly interview or deep dive plus a short "money this week" explainer. A solo "explains" segment (10 to 15 minutes, one question answered) is Brunell's best-performing format.
- Book guests from outside crypto as often as inside it: economists, fraud investigators, ex-regulators, bankers, fintech founders, journalists. They bring their own audiences and signal independence.

**Titles that work.** "Why everything costs more and keeps getting worse." "How the $6B heist actually worked." "The old system is breaking. Here's what replaces it." "Stablecoins, explained for people who don't care about crypto." "I read the bill. Here's what it does to your money."

**What to avoid.** Price predictions. Coin picks. Token sponsorships. Insider DeFi topics. Any episode where the premise is "buy this."

## 2.8 Launch playbook, Framing B: "Crypto-first, macro and sound money"

**Positioning statement.** The show for people who hold or want to hold Bitcoin and want the big picture explained without jargon or hype: why the system is breaking, what the smartest people in the space are saying, and how to protect what you own.

**Content pillars, ranked by reach**

1. **Big-name interviews framed as the future of money.** Saylor, Pal, Lyn Alden, Tom Lee, Nic Carter, ETF analysts, treasury-company executives. On crypto-native shows these do 30K to 330K; the ceiling rises sharply in a bull phase.
2. **Macro and cycle context with a point of view.** Fed policy, ETF flows, treasury adoption, where we are in the cycle. Cowen's "Where I Went Wrong" (294K) shows that honesty about being wrong outperforms certainty.
3. **The threats to Bitcoin itself.** Quantum, chain splits (BIP 110), privacy, custody failures (the Coldcard bug), state attacks. "Bitcoin is under attack" framings consistently outperform "number go up" framings on What Bitcoin Did and Coin Stories.
4. **Practical protection and self-custody.** Hardware wallets, inheritance planning, exchange risk, taxes. The sponsor-safe pillar.

**Format and cadence**

- Video-first on YouTube, audio everywhere. Two to three episodes a week: one long interview, one market-and-macro take, and optionally a short "what to do" segment. Daily shows (Wolf Of All Streets at 82 episodes a quarter) hold loyal audiences but are brutal to sustain.
- Live streams on big news days (the hack, FOMC) draw the native audience in real time.

**Titles that work.** "Who is left to sell Bitcoin?" "He did everything right and still lost his Bitcoin." "Bloomberg's top ETF analyst was a skeptic. Here's what changed his mind." "The macro shift that could send Bitcoin to $500K." "Bitcoin has 10 years to fix its biggest threat."

**What to avoid.** Altcoin picks and memecoins (the trust deficit is worst here). Paid token promotions. Daily price-call content as the brand (it is the most crowded lane and it churns). Over-promising on timelines.

**Cycle plan.** Growth in this framing follows price. Use the fear phase to build the evergreen library (explainers, self-custody guides, the big interviews) and the email list, so that when retail returns the show is already the established voice rather than one of a thousand launching into the top.
