# Crypto Podcast Audience Research: Which Lane Grows Fastest

Research date: November 1, 2026
Question: What subjects and audience lane should a new cryptocurrency podcast pick to gain followers and subscribers fastest? Same method as the AI report: adoption and sentiment surveys, Apple Podcasts charts, YouTube view comparisons by lane (scraped Nov 1, 2026), search trends, and the current market context.

## Verdict

Crypto is in a falling-attention regime right now, and that changes the answer. In AI, the open lane was "the curious general public." In crypto, the general public is not curious. It is distrustful, burned, and disengaged, and the crypto-native audience is small, loyal, and moves with price.

If you want the fastest subscriber growth of any topic, AI beats crypto clearly today. If you are committed to crypto, the lane with the most reach and the least cycle risk is **not a "crypto podcast" at all**. It is a **"money and the system" show** where Bitcoin and crypto are a recurring thread inside a bigger story: inflation, debt, the dollar, gold, stablecoins, prediction markets, scams, and the politics of money. That is the model of the only crypto-native show currently charting well (Coin Stories, #13 on Apple's US Business News chart), and it is the only crypto lane whose audience does not evaporate in a bear phase.

### 5. Monetization and risk

- Crypto YouTube CPM estimates range from $2 to $8 (bear phase) up to $10 to $30 (bull phase). Sponsorship CPMs are quoted at $40 to $300 but swing 50% to 100% with the market, and token projects often want to pay in their own tokens (Fluxnote, Creators Agency 2026).
- Crypto is a YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) category. Finance videos face demonetization risk and require disclaimers; sponsor disclosures are mandatory. Promoting a token that later collapses is a reputational and legal exposure that AI content does not carry.
- 62% of weekly podcast listeners already see AI as a credibility threat; 63% say crypto is not to be trusted. A crypto show starts with a trust deficit that an AI show does not.

## Recommended positioning (if you go crypto)

**Lane:** "Money and the system." A show about what is happening to your money, where Bitcoin and crypto are one of the answers rather than the premise. Think Coin Stories plus Prof G Markets, hosted for a normal person who is worried about inflation, distrusts banks and crypto exchanges alike, and wants someone honest to explain both.

**Content pillars, ranked by reach:**

1. **Scandals, hacks, and "how the scam worked."** The largest crypto-adjacent audience on YouTube wants to understand the heist, not buy the coin. This week's Binance hack pulled 1M+ views per video for generalist channels within 24 hours. Cover these from the outside, honestly, every time they happen.
2. **Inflation, debt, the dollar, gold, and where Bitcoin fits.** The one crypto-native angle with a non-cyclical audience. It is also the angle big general shows book (Raoul Pal, Saylor, Armstrong all get 2M to 6M on Diary of a CEO when framed as "the future of money").
3. **The new rails that are actually growing: stablecoins and prediction markets.** Stablecoins are now law and above $300B. Prediction markets doubled users in a year and are pulling in sports and politics audiences, including women, who never cared about crypto. Midterms are this week; Polymarket content is doing 400K to 680K.
4. **Practical protection:** self-custody, exchange risk, taxes (1099-DA started in 2026), avoiding scams. Smaller reach but the highest trust and the best fit for sponsors you can stand behind (hardware wallets, tax software, not tokens).

**What to avoid:**

- Price predictions and trading calls. Largest crypto-native views today, but they collapse with price, carry demonetization and reputational risk, and the audience churns to whoever called the last move.
- Insider DeFi, L2s, protocol interviews. Audience of 1K to 10K per episode and shrinking; the newsrooms serving it are closing.
- Beginner "how to buy crypto" content. The funnel is dry: 2026 beginner videos pull 2K to 34K. Save this for the next bull run, when it will matter.
- Taking token sponsorships or payment in tokens.

**Timing:** Crypto attention is cyclical. Views spike at price tops (Delphi Digital). Building a trusted "money" show now, in the fear phase, means you are the established voice when retail floods back, instead of one of a thousand channels launching into the next top. That is the counter-cyclical case for starting now. It is a slower start than AI, by design.

## Head-to-head: AI vs crypto for fastest growth

| Factor | AI | Crypto |
|---|---|---|
| Public interest trend | Rising (daily use 19% to 25% in a year; "AI" is the #4 Google search) | Falling (bitcoin searches at multi-year lows; YouTube views lowest since Jan 2021) |
| Curious non-users | 36% of adults, 47% of whom cite "don't know how" | 47% of non-owners say "never"; intent to buy fell 15% to 6% |
| 2026 beginner content | 500K to 3M views | 2K to 34K views |
| Chart presence | 8 AI shows in Apple Tech top 50; #1 is AI | 0 crypto shows in any top-50 chart |
| Trust baseline | Mixed (52% concerned vs 9% excited, but 64% use it) | Hostile (63% "not to be trusted"; 4% trust exchanges) |
| Field of competing shows | Growing | Shrinking (four major shows ended since mid-2025) |
| Monetization risk | Low | High (YMYL, token sponsors, cycle-linked CPM) |
| Growth driver you control | Point of view and format | Mostly the price cycle |

If the goal is the most followers as fast as possible, AI wins on every row. If you want both, the overlap is "AI, money, and your job," which is already the top AI pillar from the first report. A crypto segment can live inside that show without betting the brand on the cycle.

## Caveats

- YouTube view counts were scraped from YouTube search results on Nov 1, 2026 and are a snapshot, not a controlled comparison.
- vidIQ keyword and trend tools were unavailable (no credits on the connected account). Spotify's chart API returned errors; chart data is from Apple Podcasts via Podstatus and the Flexe.io study.
- Podcast download numbers are mostly unpublished. The Bankless 1M downloads a month figure comes from its own sponsor page.
- Ownership surveys disagree (14% to 42%) because of question wording and sample design. Security.org's 30% is the most cited; Gallup's 14% is the most conservative.
- The Binance hack happened this week, so the current fear reading may be an overshoot.
